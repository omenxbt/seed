How Seed works,with the math

A launchpad for raises on Solana, built on Meteora. Buy into an idea at one fixed price, paired with SOL, USDC, a tokenized stock or any token. If it misses its target by the deadline, every wallet gets back what it paid.

The rules of a raise, the Meteora programs underneath it, how pairing with any asset works, and the 1% protocol fee. Each section has the explanation on the left and the math beside it.

Overview

Seed is a launchpad for raises on Solana, built on Meteora. A creator sets a target, a deadline and the asset the raise is paired with. Backers buy in with that asset at one fixed price.

If the target is met, the raise opens a locked Meteora pool against the pair asset and backers claim their tokens. If the deadline passes first, no tokens are released and every backer takes back exactly what they paid.

Most Meteora launchpads run a bonding curve that graduates into a DAMM v2 pool. Seed keeps the same destination and replaces the curve with Meteora's Presale Vault. Arriving early does not get you a better price, so there is no reason to race and nothing for a sniper to front-run.

Lifecycle

A raise is live from the moment it opens. It leaves that state exactly once.

  • Seeded. The raise reaches the target. The vault ends the sale on the spot, even if the deadline is days away, and the pool opens.
  • Refunded. The deadline arrives and the raise is still short. No tokens are released.

While a raise is live, withdrawals are switched off in the vault. If backers could leave early, a large wallet could fake momentum and pull out at the last minute.

The buy that completes the target is trimmed to what fits. If 3.4 SOL of room is left and you send 10 SOL, the vault takes what fills it and the rest never leaves your wallet.

Price and allocation

Half of the supply is reserved for backers. The price is the target divided by that half, quoted in the pair asset, and it does not move while the raise is live.

Your tokens are your share of the raise applied to the backer allocation. Fund 1% of the target and you receive 1% of the backer half, which is 0.5% of the whole supply.

On a bonding curve the first buyer and the thousandth pay different prices for the same token. Here they pay the same, so the only question a backer has to answer is whether the idea is worth funding.

The calculator on the right uses the same formulas as the app. Switch the pair asset, then change the target or the amount to see every derived number.

Pair assets

Every raise is priced and paid for in one asset, chosen by the creator when it opens. SOL and USDC are the defaults. Any other SPL token works as well: the creator pastes its mint address.

Tokenized stocks work the same way. A raise paired with NVDAx is collected in NVDAx and opens an NVDAx pool. That gives the new token price exposure to the stock. It does not give holders any shareholder rights.

Backers buy in with that asset. The 1% fee is held in it, the Meteora pool is paired against it, and a refund returns it. Nothing is swapped along the way, so there is no slippage inside a raise.

Two things change when the pair asset is volatile. The target is fixed in units of the asset, so its dollar size moves while the raise is live. After the open, the new token's dollar price moves with the pair asset even when nobody trades it.

Check the mint. A symbol can be copied. A mint address cannot. The app marks SOL, USDC and a short list of known mints as verified. Anything else shows an Unverified tag and its full mint address in the raise panel.

Seed rejects mints that charge a transfer fee or run a transfer hook. Either one would break the rule that a refund equals what was paid.

Every formula holds in units of Q

Price, fee, pool depth and refunds are all measured in the pair asset. Dollar values follow from the pair asset's own price pQ.

What happens when it seeds

The token exists from the moment a raise opens: 1,000,000,000 supply with mint authority revoked. Half sits in the vault for backers and half waits for the pool. None of it can move until the raise resolves.

When the raise reaches the target, the vault ends the sale and Seed's launcher runs the seeding step. Anyone can trigger it, and it usually lands within seconds.

  1. The vault stops taking buys.
  2. The launcher withdraws the raise. It is the only account the vault allows to do so, and only after a fill.
  3. The whole raise is paired with the other half of the supply in a full-range Meteora DAMM v2 pool.
  4. The liquidity position is permanently locked. Nobody can withdraw it, Seed included.
  5. The 1% fees held in the vault go to the Seed treasury.
  6. Backers claim their tokens from the vault.

The fee rides on top of each buy, so the full target reaches the pool and the opening price equals the raise price exactly.

Protocol fee

Seed charges 1% of each buy. It is the Presale Vault's deposit fee, set to 100 basis points. Pay 2 SOL and 1.98 SOL counts toward the raise while 0.02 SOL is held as the fee.

The fee sits in the vault next to the funds raised. If the raise misses, the vault returns both, so a failed raise costs backers nothing beyond network fees. Seed can only collect after a raise seeds.

Because the fee rides on top of the buy, the whole target reaches the pool. A 100 SOL raise opens with 100 SOL of liquidity.

Opening a raise, claiming tokens and withdrawing a refund carry no protocol fee.

Refunds

If the deadline passes with the raise short of the target, the vault marks it failed. Each backer withdraws once and receives exactly what they paid: the amount that counted toward the raise plus the fee held on it.

Refunds are pulled by backers instead of pushed by the program. A push loop over hundreds of wallets does not fit in a Solana transaction, and one bad account could stall it. With pull withdrawals, every refund is independent of every other.

Withdrawals do not expire. The supply is pulled out of the vault and burned, so a failed token never reaches a wallet. Each buy creates a small escrow account, and the rent paid for it comes back with the withdrawal. The full cost of a missed raise to a backer is two network fees.

Refund per wallet

refundi = ci + feei = gi

Conservation

Σ refundi = Σ gi

The vault holds every unit that was paid and owes every unit back. After the last withdrawal its balance is zero.

After it opens

Once seeded, the token trades in a full-range Meteora DAMM v2 pool, which is a constant-product market. The pool's depth is set by the raise: a 100 SOL raise opens with 100 SOL on the pair side.

Backers together hold as many tokens as the pool does. The pool cannot buy all of them back at the raise price. Selling moves the price down along the curve, and the first seller gets a better price than the last.

The table on the right shows what a backer recovers, as a share of what they paid, by selling their whole allocation at the open. It assumes nobody else has traded and leaves out the pool's own swap fee.

A refund covers a raise that fails. It does not cover a token that falls after it opens.

Reading the terminal

The terminal lists every raise, whatever it is paired with, along with the numbers needed to judge whether it will make its deadline.

  • Filled is raised over target, counting buys net of the fee.
  • Pace needed is how much has to arrive per hour, from now until the deadline, for the raise to seed. Compare it with the slope of the fill chart. When the dashed line is steeper than the solid one, the raise is behind.
  • Quick deposit sends your preset amount in one click. Presets are kept per asset, so a SOL raise uses your SOL preset and a USDC raise uses your USDC preset. The preset is what you pay, fee included. Like any buy, it is trimmed to what fits.

Clicking a raise opens its own page in a new tab, with the fill chart, a custom amount field and the most recent buys. The terminal stays open where you left it, and the two tabs keep each other up to date.

Built on Meteora

Seed does not run its own raise contract. It is assembled from two Meteora programs, the same way curve launchpads are assembled from Meteora's bonding curve and DAMM v2.

Presale Vault holds the raise and the backer half of the supply. Seed configures it as a fixed-price sale with withdrawals off and an early end at the cap. The refund rule lives in that program, not in ours.

DAMM v2 is the pool. The raise and the other half of the supply go in across the full price range, and the position is permanently locked. Aggregators such as Jupiter can route to it as soon as it opens.

Seed's launcher is the glue. It creates the token and the vault in the one transaction a creator signs. After a fill it moves the raise into the pool and locks it. It is the only account the vault lets withdraw a successful raise, and it cannot touch a live or failed one.

Any Solana wallet works, including Phantom, Solflare and Backpack.

Risks

  • Price after the open. The refund guarantee ends when a raise seeds. From then on the token is a market asset and can lose value.
  • Locked funds. A buy cannot be reversed while the raise is live. Your funds are tied up until it resolves, which can take the full deadline.
  • Pair asset. A refund returns the asset you paid with, not its dollar value on the day you bought. If the pair asset falls while the raise is live, so does the value of your refund.
  • Look-alike mints. Anyone can create a token called USDC. Buy only when the pair asset is verified or you have checked the mint yourself.
  • Tokenized stocks. Their issuer can pause or freeze them. A raise or pool paired with one inherits that.
  • The seeding step. Between a raise filling and its pool opening, the funds pass through Seed's launcher. It takes seconds, and it is the one point where backers rely on Seed instead of on Meteora's programs.
  • Creators. The vault enforces the raise rules. It does not enforce that a creator builds what they described.
  • Programs. Smart contract risk applies here as it does to any onchain protocol. Meteora's Presale Vault is still in beta.
