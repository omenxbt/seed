How Seed works,in plain terms

A launchpad for raises on Solana. Buy into an idea at one fixed price, paired with SOL or USDC. If it fills, the whole raise opens a Meteora pool that is locked for good. If it misses, every wallet gets back exactly what it paid.

The rules of a raise, the supply split, the two fees, team unlocks, what Seed enforces and what it leaves to you. Each section has the explanation on the left and the numbers that matter beside it.

Overview

A founder connects a wallet, sets a target of up to $100,000, a deadline of one to fourteen days, the asset the raise is paired with, and how much of the supply the team keeps. Backers buy in with that asset at one fixed price, plus a 1% fee on top.

If the target is met, Seed's program mints the 1,000,000,000 supply, revokes the mint and freeze authorities, puts the whole raise and the pool's half of the supply into a Meteora DAMM v2 pool at the raise price, and locks that liquidity permanently. Backers claim their tokens straight away. If the deadline passes first, nothing is minted and every backer takes back what they paid, fee included.

There is no treasury, no vault and no reserve. The money raised goes into the pool and nowhere else, so there is nothing to rug. What a founder earns is a share of the pool's trading fees, paid to a payout wallet, and the team tokens they chose to keep, which unlock on a schedule everyone can see.

Seed enforces a short list of hard rules in its program and acts on fraud. Everything else a founder decides, and the page shows those decisions as facts rather than warnings.

Lifecycle

A raise is live from the moment it opens, and it leaves that state exactly once.

  • Filled. The raise reaches the target. It closes to buys on the spot, even if the deadline is days away, and seeding begins: four idempotent steps that anyone can run. If they are not all done within three days, the raise refunds instead.
  • Seeded. The last step lands. The token exists, the pool is open and locked, backers claim their tokens, and the team's unlock clock starts.
  • Refunded. The deadline arrives and the raise is short, the founder cancels it, or seeding times out. Nothing is minted and every backer claims back what they paid.

While a raise is live, buys cannot be withdrawn. If backers could leave early, a large wallet could fake momentum and pull out at the last minute. A founder can cancel a live raise, which refunds everyone the same way a miss does.

The buy that completes the target is trimmed to what fits. If 3.4 SOL of room is left and you send 10 SOL, the program takes what fills it and the rest never leaves your wallet.

Price and the supply split

The founder chooses the team share. Whatever the team does not take is split equally between backers and the pool. The price is the target divided by the backers' tokens, quoted in the pair asset, and it does not move while the raise is live.

Because backers and the pool hold the same number of tokens, and the pool opens with the whole target against them, the pool opens at exactly the price backers paid. That holds for any team share.

Your tokens are your share of the raise applied to the backer allocation. Fund 1% of the target and you receive 1% of the backers' tokens.

The founder is not asked to justify the team share or its unlock schedule; both are shown on the page as they are, next to the price they imply. A bigger team share means fewer tokens for backers and the pool at the same target, which is the same as a higher fully diluted value.

Pair assets and the cap

Every raise is priced and paid for in one asset, chosen by the founder when it opens: SOL or USDC. Both are deep, verified and simple to price, which is why they are the only two.

Backers buy in with that asset. The fee is held in it, the Meteora pool is paired against it, the pool's fees are collected in it, and a refund returns it. Nothing is swapped along the way, so there is no slippage inside a raise.

A target can be at most $100,000. A USDC target is its own dollar size. A SOL target is converted at the SOL price the moment the raise opens, and that is the number the cap is checked against; the dollar size then moves with SOL while the raise is live, and after the open the token's dollar price moves with SOL even when nobody trades it.

Everything is in the pair asset

Price, fees, pool depth and refunds are all measured in the pair asset. Dollar values follow from the pair asset's own price at the open, and the $100,000 cap is checked then.

What happens when it fills

Nothing is minted while a raise is live. The buys sit in a program-owned escrow until the raise fills or refunds. When the target lands, the raise closes to buys and seeding begins: four steps, each one idempotent, each one a transaction anyone can send. Seed's own scheduler sends them too, so in practice they land within a minute.

  1. Filled. The raise is marked full. Nothing else can change it.
  2. Minted. The 1,000,000,000 supply is minted into program-owned accounts and the mint and freeze authorities are revoked. Nobody, Seed included, can mint more or freeze a wallet.
  3. Pooled. The whole raise and the pool's tokens open a full-range Meteora DAMM v2 pool at the raise price. The liquidity position is locked permanently, and its NFT is held by a Seed program account that no instruction can move it out of.
  4. Seeded. Backers' claims open and the team's unlock clock starts.

If the four steps are not all done within three days of filling, the raise refunds. That is the only way a filled raise fails, and it needs no one's permission: the timeout is in the program.

The fee rides on top of each buy, so the full target reaches the pool and the opening price equals the raise price exactly.

The two fees

Seed's fee on buys. 1% of each buy, charged on top. Put 2 SOL into a raise and you pay 2.02 SOL. The fee is held beside the raise until it resolves: if the raise seeds, it goes to Seed; if it refunds, it goes back to you with the buy. A failed raise costs backers nothing beyond network fees.

The pool's fee on swaps. The DAMM v2 pool charges a flat 1.25% on every trade, collected in the pair asset only. Meteora's protocol keeps 20% of that, so 1% of volume reaches the locked position. At seeding the split is fixed: 70% of what the position earns goes to the founder's payout wallet and 30% to Seed. The split cannot be changed afterwards, not by the founder and not by Seed.

Claiming is permissionless. Anyone can send the claim transaction and the money still goes where the snapshot says. Seed also claims on a schedule, so a founder never has to. This is a founder's income from a raise: not the raise itself, which is all in the pool, but a share of what trading on it earns.

Opening a raise, claiming tokens, claiming fees and withdrawing a refund carry no Seed fee.

Refunds

A raise refunds in three cases: the deadline passes with the raise short of the target, the founder cancels it while it is live, or it fills but seeding does not finish within three days. In every case the result is the same. Each backer withdraws once and receives exactly what they paid: the amount that went into the raise plus the fee held on it.

Refunds are pulled by backers instead of pushed by the program. A push loop over hundreds of wallets does not fit in a Solana transaction, and one bad account could stall it. With pull withdrawals, every refund is independent of every other.

Withdrawals do not expire. Nothing was minted, so a failed token never reaches a wallet. Each buy creates a small escrow account, and the rent paid for it comes back with the withdrawal. The full cost of a missed raise to a backer is two network fees.

What comes back

Exactly what you paid: the buy and the fee held on it. The escrow holds every unit that was paid and owes every unit back; after the last withdrawal its balance is zero.

Team tokens and unlocks

The founder sets the team share before the raise opens, anywhere from nothing to just under the whole supply, and a schedule for how those tokens unlock. The schedule is either a cliff followed by the same slice every N days, or a list of dated tranches. Backers see both on the raise page, with a chart, before they buy.

Two rules are enforced by the program: the slices must add up to 100%, and the last unlock must be at least 90 days after seeding. Everything else is the founder's call. The schedule is written into the raise at seeding and cannot be changed after that.

Unlocked tokens are claimed to the payout wallet, by the founder or by anyone else; they go to the payout wallet either way. If a community takeover has moved the payout wallet, the remaining unlocks follow it.

Backers' tokens are not vested. They are claimable the moment the raise seeds.

After it opens

Once seeded, the token trades in a full-range Meteora DAMM v2 pool, which is a constant-product market. The pool's depth is set by the raise: a 100 SOL raise opens with 100 SOL on the pair side.

Backers together hold as many tokens as the pool does. The pool cannot buy all of them back at the raise price. Selling moves the price down along the curve, and the first seller gets a better price than the last. Team tokens, once unlocked, add to what can be sold.

The table on the right shows what a backer recovers, as a share of what they paid, by selling their whole allocation at the open. It assumes nobody else has traded and leaves out the pool's own swap fee.

A refund covers a raise that fails. It does not cover a token that falls after it opens.

Selling into the pool

The pool opens with the whole target on one side and as many tokens as backers hold on the other. Every sale pushes the price down a little, so a backer who sells everything at the open gets back a bit less than they paid, and more so the larger their share of the raise.

Founders, reports and fraud

A wallet is enough; X is optional. A founder opens a raise with a connected wallet. There is no KYC. A wallet can have one live raise at a time, and a wallet flagged for fraud cannot open another. Linking an X account, at opening or later, adds a verified badge: the account shows on the raise page with its age and follower count. The link is checked by the program: Seed's backend signs a short-lived attestation that ties the wallet to the account, and the instruction verifies that signature on chain. A raise without one shows nothing more than that none is linked.

The payout wallet. Creator fees and team unlocks go to the payout wallet the founder set at opening. A founder can move it, and the change takes effect seven days after the request; the raise page shows the pending change for the whole week.

Reports. Anyone with a wallet can report a raise. Reports are signed by the wallet, kept off chain and shown publicly on the raise page. Seed acts on fraud; it does not referee disagreements about whether a roadmap is on time.

What admins can do. Seed's admin multisig has exactly two instructions. Flag fraud marks the raise, blacklists the founder wallet from opening another (and the X account, when one is linked), and disables buying through Seed. Approve a takeover moves the payout wallet to one the backers chose, so creator fees and the remaining unlocks go to the community instead of the founder. Neither touches the pool, which stays locked, or backers' tokens, which are already theirs.

What admins cannot do. Withdraw liquidity, mint tokens, change a fee split, change a schedule, or take a refund. None of those instructions exist.

Reading the terminal

The terminal lists every raise, paired with SOL or USDC, in three columns by stage: New raises (under 90% filled), Almost full (90% and over, or filled and seeding) and Seeded & trading. Trending sits above them: the eight most active raises, whatever the filters say. Each row carries the numbers needed to judge whether it will make its deadline.

  • Filled is raised over target. Fees ride on top, so they are not part of it.
  • Pace compares the last six hours of buys with what is left and the time to the deadline. When the dashed line on the fill chart is steeper than the solid one, the raise is behind.
  • Seeding shows a filled raise's step count and the time left before it would refund.
  • Price on a seeded raise is the pool's, with its change since the raise price.
  • Quick deposit sends your preset amount in one click, fee on top. Presets are kept per asset. Like any buy, it is trimmed to what fits.

Finding a raise. Search matches a name, a ticker, a token address, a raise account or an X handle. Filters narrow the columns by pair asset, Verified X and the safety chips. The star on a row adds it to your watchlist; the Watchlist toggle shows only starred raises. The bell collects alerts for the raises you star, back, hold or opened: filled, seeded, refunds open, a team unlock passed, a payout wallet moved. The watchlist and the alerts live in this browser.

Clicking a raise opens its own page in a new tab, with the fill chart, the team's unlocks, the founder (and the X account, when one is linked), the info panel and the reports. The terminal stays open where you left it, and the two tabs keep each other up to date.

Once it is seeded the page becomes the token's: a live price chart (price or market cap, 1m to 1D, with the raise price marked), the trade panel on the raise's Meteora pool (buy and sell with quick amounts, slippage, a priority fee and the price impact before you sign), the trades tape, the holders with Seed's own accounts labelled (the locked pool, backers' unclaimed tokens, team vesting), the top traders and their results, the creator fees and what has been claimed, and the safety checks read from the chain: mint and freeze authority, the liquidity lock, metadata immutability and holder concentration.

The founder's page (from the Founder card) lists every raise a wallet opened and how each ended, its fee income and claims, and its team unlocks. Portfolio is your own record: open positions, results by day, claims waiting (tokens or refunds), the history of everything you did on Seed, and for founders a Creator tab with fees, unlocks and the payout wallet.

The program and Meteora

Seed's program holds the raise. It is an Anchor program with one config account and, per raise, a raise account, an escrow for the buys, a contribution account per backer, and a program-owned account that holds the pool position after seeding. It takes buys, enforces the cap, the deadline, the refunds and the unlock rules, runs the four seeding steps, splits the pool's fees on claim, and carries the two admin instructions. Nothing in it can move the locked position.

DAMM v2 is the pool. The raise and the pool's tokens go in across the full price range with the pair asset as token B, the fee mode set to collect in token B only, a flat 1.25% fee with the dynamic fee and the fee scheduler off, and the position locked at creation. Aggregators such as Jupiter can route to it as soon as it opens.

Off chain, a Supabase backend indexes the program's events for the site, signs the X attestations, keeps reports and announcements, and runs the schedules that crank seeding and claim fees. None of it holds funds.

On devnet the program's upgrade authority is a developer key. On mainnet it moves to a Squads multisig. Any Solana wallet works, including Phantom, Solflare and Backpack.

Risks

  • Price after the open. The refund guarantee ends when a raise seeds. From then on the token is a market asset and can lose value.
  • Team tokens. A founder can sell team tokens as they unlock. The share and the schedule are on the page before you buy; read them as part of the price.
  • Locked funds. A buy cannot be reversed while the raise is live. Your funds are tied up until it resolves, which can take the full deadline plus three days of seeding.
  • Pair asset. A refund returns the asset you paid with, not its dollar value on the day you bought. If the pair asset falls while the raise is live, so does the value of your refund.
  • Founders. The program enforces the raise rules. It does not enforce that a founder builds what they described. Reports and the two admin instructions are the recourse, and neither returns your money.
  • Admins. A flag or a takeover is a judgement call by Seed's multisig. It can be wrong in either direction.
  • Programs. Smart contract risk applies here as it does to any onchain protocol, to Seed's program and to Meteora's.
